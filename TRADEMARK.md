# Trademark policy

Written before anyone had a reason to fork, which is the only time such a policy
can be written in good faith. Godot was still drafting its policy months after
Redot forked, under exactly the pressure that makes fair terms hard.

## The code is yours. The name is ours.

The software is MIT: fork it, sell it, embed it, build a business on it. No
permission needed and none should be asked.

**"Folio" and the Folio marks are not covered by the MIT licence**, and are held
by Mochi Labs LLC.

## What you may do without asking

- Say your project "runs on Folio", "is built with Folio", or "is Folio-compatible"
- Say your game is "a Folio game" or ships as "a .folio"
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, and reviews
- Publish a package named `folio-<something>` that genuinely works with Folio
- **Claim conformance** if you pass the conformance suite — that is what it is for,
  and an independent implementation passing it is a good day for this project

## What needs a conversation first

- Naming a **fork** "Folio" or something confusingly close to it
- Naming a **commercial product or service** "Folio"
- Using the marks in a way that implies we built, endorsed, or support your thing

Fork the code freely; just give the fork its own name, so nobody downloads yours
believing it is ours.

## The format is not the trademark

The `.folio` file extension, the format specification, and the World State
Contract are open and unencumbered. Write your own reader. Write your own engine.
Ship `.folio` files. That is the point of specifying them in public.
